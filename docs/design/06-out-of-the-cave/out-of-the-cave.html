Out of the Cave

Every session walks out of Plato's cave: it starts in the dark with what you feel sure of and ends in daylight with what you could prove. This version follows Apple's design principles and the research on reading comfort, navigation and learning.

What the research changed

PrincipleWhat changedSource
Agency: a guided flow should be easy to leaveEvery session screen has Close, and closing saves your answers.Apple HIG
Responsibility: be clear about what the product doesThe welcome says the tutor is an AI that never hands over the answer.Apple HIG
Liquid Glass: controls float above content; brand color stays in contentClose, Skip, Done and the answer box are glass. Bronze marks only the main action.Apple HIG
Typography: 17 pt text, no light weights, a brand face for headlines and the system face for controlsNewsreader for titles, questions and the conversation, at 17 pt with roomy lines. Buttons, labels and lists use the phone's own font.Apple HIG
Harmony: capsules and nested cornersCapsule buttons, and every inner corner is the outer corner minus the padding.WWDC25
Contrast: aim for 7:1 for small textSecondary text darkened to 7.6:1. Hints moved from bright gold to a soft container.Apple HIG
Dark mode: dark grey, never black; muted accentsDark mode uses warm umber grey and a muted gold for hints.Material
Response times: anything over a second needs a moving indicator and a reason"Checking your answer…" with moving dots while the tutor replies, and Send waits.NN/g
Errors: say what happened where it happened, keep the input, offer a fixA failed reply shows under your answer, keeps it, and offers Try Again. No red: terracotta only means the gap.NN/g
Navigation: tab bars are for 3 to 5 peer sectionsA home screen instead of a tab bar, with Progress one tap away and settings in the corner.Material
Goal-gradient, Zeigarnik: visible progress and unfinished work pull people backThe Greek key shows the parts all session, and Home leads with Resume.Laws of UX
Progressive disclosure: one main thing per screenAt level 5 the map fills the screen and the fact desk is a half-height sheet.NN/g
Help with a visible price: learners try first, see the cost, and chooseThe cost is on the Help button, and hints never open on their own.Koedinger & Aleven
Competence: levels need clear criteriaProgress says what moves a part up a stage.Deci et al.
Peak-end: the ending shapes the memoryThe result casts each point into bronze one by one, then gives one next step.Laws of UX
Onboarding: teach by doingOne skippable welcome. Hints and level 5 explain themselves when they first appear.Apple HIG
