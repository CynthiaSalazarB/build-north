What we know about the problem and the market as of Oct 21, 2026, and what it doesn't prove yet. The
reasoning built on it is in the [product thesis](../synthesis/product-thesis.md).

## Learners describe the problem

**A reel about studying with coding agents without understanding** ([Instagram, July 2026](https://www.instagram.com/reel/DbCQcp6TQpR/))
drew about 200 comments, mostly from programming students and junior developers, writing in Spanish.
Read in full on Oct 20; paraphrased here, no usernames.

- More than 15 people described a workaround they already use. About five are close to what this app
  does, done by hand, with the learner doing the explaining and the AI asking or correcting: explain the
  concept to the AI and let it correct you; have it ask why you did each thing; ask it for
  interview-style questions about your own project, then find the gaps; teach the concept back in
  simple words (the Feynman technique).
- The most-liked workaround (113 likes) is the passive one: have the AI explain everything while it
  builds. The AI still does the explaining.
- Costs people already paid: a student scolded for not being able to explain their own final project;
  a developer who says that after relying on AI, when they try to think on their own, nothing comes out.
- A former programming instructor quit at the end of a semester: their students handed work in fast but
  couldn't defend it.
- Even the skeptics ("you don't need to write code anymore") say the concepts still matter, to direct the AI.

**On Reddit**, a senior developer's post (about 830 upvotes, read through a copy on an archive site)
says many junior
candidates can't write simple code without AI or explain their own code when asked. A high school
student describes building a website with AI and no longer being able to work on it without AI.

## Buyers already pay to check understanding

- Colleges are turning to oral exams because written work no longer shows who understood it (AP,
  April 2026, "Perfect homework, blank stares"). A UC San Diego professor ran a three-year study on
  scaling oral exams and now runs workshops for other universities. Institutions are paying in staff
  hours to verify understanding.

## Research on learning with AI

- **Bastani et al., PNAS 2025.** Close to 1,000 high school math students. With unrestricted GPT-4,
  practice grades went up 48%, but once access was taken away their exam grades were 17% lower than
  those of students who never had it. A version with guardrails, told to give hints instead of
  answers, removed most of the harm but didn't beat the students without AI on the exam. Short-term
  outcomes only.
- **Aalto University, Computers in Human Behavior 2025** (senior author Robin Welsch). Two studies, 246 and 452
  people, solving logical-reasoning problems (from the LSAT, the US law school admission test), half
  with ChatGPT. The ChatGPT users scored better but overestimated their score by about 4 points, and
  the more AI literate they rated themselves, the more overconfident they were. Most asked the AI only
  one question per problem. Figures from the university's summary; the full paper wasn't read.

## Competitors

| Product | What it does | Where it stops |
|---|---|---|
| [ChatGPT Study Mode](https://campustechnology.com/Articles/2025/07/30/New-ChatGPT-Study-Mode-Guides-Students-Through-Questions.aspx) (Jul 2025) | Socratic questions (questions that lead the student toward the answer instead of giving it), hints and progress checks; free | a mode the student can switch off; open chat, no structure to build, no score |
| [Gemini Guided Learning](https://techcrunch.com/2025/08/06/google-takes-on-chatgpts-study-mode-with-new-guided-learning-tool-in-gemini/) (Aug 2025) | breaks problems into steps, asks follow-up questions, quizzes | same as Study Mode |
| [Claude Code Learning output style](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/output-styles) | inside the coding tool, leaves `TODO(human)` pieces for the developer to write | practice writing code in one project, not understanding a concept |
| CS50 Duck (Harvard), Khanmigo | tutors with guardrails that don't hand over answers | tied to one course or platform |

A search on Oct 21 found no startup doing exactly this for students who build with AI agents.

## Prices in the category

- Codecademy Pro: [$19.99 a month billed yearly, $39.99 monthly](https://scrimba.com/articles/best-boot-dev-alternatives-2026/).
- Boot.dev: [$59 a month or $399 a year](https://www.boot.dev/blog/education/bootdev-vs-codecademy).
- Khanmigo for school districts: [$15 per student](https://blog.khanacademy.org/?p=18400), Khan Academy's own figure.

These show that learners and schools pay in this category, not that they would pay for this app.

## What this evidence doesn't show

- Comments and upvotes are agreement, not commitment. Nobody has used or paid for the app yet.
- The comments come from one creator's audience, reacting to a feeling. None of them are high school
  students; for that group we rely on the research above.
- Whether the app improves understanding more than a study mode or no tool at all. The Oct 23 pilot
  (5 testers, each tested on a new concept before and after training) is the first small signal, not
  proof.
